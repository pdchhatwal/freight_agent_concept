Design review · SHP-2041 run screen

Your screen shows what the agent did. An agentic UI also has to show why it did it, what it does next, and where you come in.

The bones are good: a stated goal, a plan with tools per step, company rules beside the run, and an evidence log. Right now it reads like a workflow dashboard with a chat box attached. The changes below move it toward a place where one person can supervise many agents.

Keep these

The goal in plain words"Book and forward SHP-2041 to vendor, notify customer" is the right anchor for every run.
A tool on every step"rate APIs", "vendor API", "tracking MCP" tell people what the agent can touch.
Company rules beside the runSeeing policy next to the action is how people learn to trust it.
Eligibility is visible"Auto-book eligible" is the start of a real autonomy contract.

What's holding it back

Sorted by how much each one costs you in operator trust. The first one is a data bug, and it is the most important.

Critical

The carrier decision doesn't add up on screen

Follow the carrier through the page and it changes three times. FastFreight's own price never appears, so nobody can check the choice.

Header$1,240 · FastFreight
Step 2FedEx $1,240 · DHL $1,380 · no FastFreight quote
Evidence 09:14"Selected FedEx $1,240 via carrier skill"
Step 3Forward to FastFreight
Brain rule"prefer FastFreight on SG→MY" when delta <5%, probably the real reason, but nothing links it to step 3

FixGive every decision a receipt: the options considered, the rule that fired, the arithmetic, and what would have happened without the rule. See concept 2.

High

It's a run viewer, not a place to supervise

All five runs get the same weight in the list. The one that is actually blocked on a person, SHP-2037 Needs gate, sits second and looks like the others. With 50 runs a day, the operator's real question is "what needs me, and by when?"

FixMake the home screen a queue of decisions ranked by deadline, with the agent's recommendation and a one-key answer on each. Show everything else as a fleet at a glance. See concept 1.

High

You can't see what happens next

Steps 4 and 5 are only labels. What will the customer email say, and when will it go out? What happens if FastFreight doesn't accept within the 15-minute SLA? That SLA only shows up inside a log line.

FixShow future steps with predicted times, drafts you can read now, live timers, and the fallback the agent will take on its own. See concept 2.

Medium

Four controls for one mode, and the mode doesn't say what you may do

The In flight badge, the Switch to preview link, the In flight / Preview toggle and the In flight — surgical overrides only banner all describe the same state. "Surgical overrides" doesn't say which overrides are allowed.

FixShow one status. Then mark each step with what you can do there: edit it, pause before it, swap the carrier, or nothing because it already happened.

Medium

Override takes you away from the context

override ↗ opens somewhere else at the moment you most need the quotes, the rule and the clock in view. You also can't see what an override will change before you make it.

FixIntervene in place. Before anything runs, show a preview of what the override changes, for example "Cancels FastFreight request, re-runs step 3 with FedEx, email unchanged".

Medium

The company brain is a static list

It shows three rules but not which ones fired in this run. It never learns from what people correct. If Ravi swaps FedEx for FastFreight every week, the brain should notice and propose a rule.

FixLink rules and decisions both ways. Turn repeated human corrections into proposed rules, backtested on past runs before anyone accepts them. See concept 3.

Medium

Evidence lives in a separate log

The same facts appear twice, once in the plan and once in the evidence panel, worded differently. That is how the FedEx/FastFreight mismatch slipped in.

FixAttach evidence to the step it proves. Keep the full log one click away for audits.

Low

The chat box has no scope and no preview

Ask about this run, or give the agent an instruction… doesn't say whether an instruction applies to this step, this run or every SG→MY run, or what it will change.

FixLet instructions carry a scope. Have the agent repeat back its understanding as a change preview. Let answers come back as UI, such as a comparison table or a list of affected runs, instead of paragraphs. See concept 4.

Low

Small things

The filter chips (▷ ⚠ ✓) have no labels or counts. "Gate to Ravi" names a person but doesn't say whether Ravi is available or who covers. "Auto-book eligible" is the only sign of autonomy, and it can't be adjusted.

Six principles the concepts follow

Attention over activityLead with what needs a human and the deadline for it. The agent's busywork is summary, not headline.
Every decision has a receiptOptions considered, rule applied, arithmetic, confidence, and what changes if you reverse it.
Show the futurePredicted steps, drafts, timers and fallbacks are visible before they happen, not after.
Steer without stoppingBreakpoints, pinned instructions and undo windows let you shape a run while it keeps moving.
Autonomy is earned and adjustableEach action type has a level and a track record. The agent asks to be promoted with evidence.
Corrections become knowledgeRepeated overrides turn into proposed rules, backtested on real runs, with a versioned history.

One colour rule across every concept

On a screen where a machine and people both act, the first question is always "who did this?" Each concept answers it with the same three marks.

AgentDid, decided or drafted it
Company brainA written rule shaped it
PersonSomeone on your team set or changed it

Four concepts

Each one is a working prototype with sample data. Buttons, timers and keyboard shortcuts behave the way they would in the product.

01

Attention inbox

The home screen. Decisions waiting on people, ranked by deadline, with a fleet view of everything the agent handled alone.

  • Answer a card with 1 2 3
  • Watch the deadlines count down
  • Spot-check the agent's solo work
 02

Run trajectory

SHP-2041 rebuilt. Past, live and planned steps on one line, a decision receipt for each, and breakpoints like a debugger.

  • Open the step 2 receipt
  • Toggle the breakpoint before the email
  • Type "use DHL if no accept in 5 min"
 03

Delegation

What the agent may do without asking, the track record behind each level, and rules learned from your corrections.

  • Promote an action and see the blast radius
  • Backtest a proposed rule
  • Read the brain's version history
 04

Ops thread

A conversation where answers come back as working UI: comparison tables, affected-run lists and drafts, all previewed before they run.

  • Ask "what's at risk today?"
  • Hold Jakarta shipments as a dry run
  • Answer the agent's own question
